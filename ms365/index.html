🔍 Architecture & Microsoft Graph

Microsoft 365 Copilot is not a standalone AI model — it is an orchestration system that combines a large language model (GPT-4 class via Azure OpenAI) with your organization's data, accessed through Microsoft Graph, to produce grounded, contextual responses.

The M365 Copilot Stack

  • Large Language Model: Azure OpenAI GPT-4 class — handles natural language understanding, generation, and reasoning.
  • Microsoft Graph: The unified API layer that retrieves relevant content from your M365 data estate and injects it into the LLM prompt as grounding context.
  • Semantic Index: Microsoft's proprietary enterprise search index — pre-computed embeddings of your M365 content (emails, documents, chats) that enable semantic (meaning-based) retrieval, not just keyword search.
  • Microsoft 365 Apps: Outlook, Word, Excel, Teams, PowerPoint, SharePoint — each surfaces Copilot capabilities native to that app's context.
  • Microsoft Purview: Data classification and sensitivity labels are respected by Copilot — it won't surface content the user doesn't have permission to access.

How Microsoft Graph Powers Copilot

When you ask Copilot a question, here's what happens before the LLM generates a response:

  1. Intent analysis: The orchestration layer interprets your query and determines what types of M365 data are relevant.
  2. Graph queries: Microsoft Graph is called to retrieve relevant content: emails matching the query context, recent files from OneDrive/SharePoint, relevant Teams messages, calendar events, meeting transcripts.
  3. Semantic ranking: The Semantic Index ranks retrieved content by semantic relevance to your query — not just recency or keyword match.
  4. Prompt assembly: Relevant content snippets are assembled into the LLM prompt as context, alongside your original query and any app-specific instructions.
  5. LLM response: The model generates a response grounded in the retrieved content, with citations where appropriate.
💡 Permission boundary: Copilot strictly respects M365 permissions. It can only retrieve content you have access to — it won't surface files shared with different departments or emails from other users' mailboxes. The Semantic Index is permission-aware.

What Microsoft Graph Retrieves

  • Exchange Online: Emails, calendar events, meeting details, contacts
  • SharePoint & OneDrive: Files (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF), SharePoint pages, lists
  • Microsoft Teams: Chat messages, channel posts, meeting transcripts, meeting recordings metadata
  • Planner & To Do: Tasks and assignments
  • Microsoft Loop: Collaborative content blocks

📱 Per-App Capabilities

📧 Copilot in Outlook

Copilot in Outlook focuses on email productivity — drafting, summarizing, and analyzing email threads.

  • Draft with Copilot: Provide a few bullet points or a brief description, and Copilot generates a full email. Adjust the tone (direct, casual, formal) and length after generation.
  • Thread Summary: For long email chains, Copilot generates a summary of the conversation, key decisions made, and open action items — useful for catching up on threads without reading every reply.
  • Coaching: After drafting an email, ask Copilot to evaluate it for clarity, sentiment, and impact. It suggests specific rewrites.
  • Scheduling intelligence: Copilot in Outlook can reference meeting transcripts and calendar context to help schedule follow-ups relevant to prior conversations.
⚠️ Thread summaries work best on threads under ~100 messages. Very long threads may be truncated — Copilot will indicate when this occurs.

📝 Copilot in Word

Copilot in Word is most powerful when used with reference files — it can synthesize content from multiple M365 sources into a draft document.

  • Draft from prompt: "Write a product requirements document for a mobile expense tracking app aimed at field service teams" — Copilot generates a structured draft with sections, headings, and content.
  • Reference files: Use the / slash command to reference specific OneDrive or SharePoint files. Copilot pulls relevant content from those files into the document — "Draft an executive summary referencing /Q4-Results.xlsx and /Strategy-Deck.pptx."
  • Transform selected text: Select any text in the document and ask Copilot to rewrite, summarize, expand, or change tone without regenerating the entire document.
  • Meeting-to-doc: "Draft a project kickoff document based on the notes from my Teams meeting with [client] yesterday" — pulls from meeting transcript via Graph.

📊 Copilot in Excel

Copilot in Excel bridges the gap between natural language questions and spreadsheet analysis — without requiring formula knowledge.

  • Natural language analysis: Ask questions in plain English: "Which product category had the highest revenue growth from Q1 to Q2?" — Copilot runs the analysis and returns results with a supporting chart or table.
  • Formula generation: Describe what you want to calculate, and Copilot writes the formula — with an explanation of each component. It can handle complex formulas including XLOOKUP, dynamic arrays, and LAMBDA functions.
  • Conditional formatting suggestions: "Highlight cells where profit margin falls below 15%" — Copilot identifies the appropriate range and creates the formatting rule.
  • Data insights: Copilot proactively identifies trends, outliers, and patterns in your selected data range without requiring a specific question.
  • Limitation: Requires data to be in a structured Excel Table (not just a range). Use Ctrl+T to convert ranges before working with Copilot.

🎯 Copilot in Teams

  • In-meeting Copilot: During a live meeting with transcription enabled, Copilot can answer "What have we covered so far?" or "What are the points of disagreement?" in real time.
  • Post-meeting recap: Automatically generates a structured summary: discussion topics, key decisions, action items with owners, and a follow-up email draft.
  • Channel catch-up: "What did the team discuss in the #engineering channel this week?" — Copilot summarizes channel activity you missed.
  • Meeting prep: Before joining, ask "What are the key topics and attendees for my 2pm meeting?" — Copilot pulls calendar context, recent emails with those attendees, and relevant files.

📊 Copilot in PowerPoint

  • Create from scratch: "Create a 10-slide presentation on the benefits of zero-trust security for enterprise IT teams" — generates a full deck with title slide, content slides, and speaker notes.
  • Create from Word doc: Reference a Word document and Copilot builds a presentation from its structure — maintaining heading hierarchy as slide sections.
  • Summarize a long deck: "Summarize this 40-slide deck into 5 key points" — useful for consuming large decks quickly or creating executive summaries.
  • Organize: "Reorganize this deck so the problem statement comes before the solution" — Copilot restructures slide order based on narrative logic.

💬 Copilot Chat (BizChat)

Microsoft Copilot Chat (formerly BizChat) is the cross-app conversational AI interface for M365 Copilot, accessible at copilot.microsoft.com, within Microsoft Teams, and in the Windows 11 taskbar. It's the "universal" Copilot entry point — not scoped to a single app.

Work Mode vs. Web Mode

Copilot Chat operates in two distinct modes:

Mode Data Sources Privacy Best For
Work (Protected) Your M365 tenant data via Graph Enterprise data boundary — not used for training Org-specific queries, sensitive content
Web Public internet (Bing search) Standard Microsoft Privacy Policy General research, public information

Cross-App Intelligence Examples

Copilot Chat's key differentiator is cross-app reasoning — it can synthesize data from multiple M365 sources in a single response:

  • "Summarize everything I've received about Project Alpha this week — emails, Teams messages, and meeting notes."
  • "Who are the key stakeholders for the Q1 budget review, and when did I last communicate with them?"
  • "Find all files shared by [colleague] in the last month that mention 'compliance' or 'audit'."
  • "Draft a status update for my manager based on my Teams activity and the tasks in my Planner this week."

Copilot Studio Extension Integration

Copilot Chat can invoke custom Copilot Studio plugins as extensions — routing relevant queries to org-specific copilots built by your IT team. When a user's query matches the description of an installed plugin, the M365 Copilot orchestration layer automatically routes the request to that plugin. This enables enterprise-specific capabilities (HR policy lookup, IT ticket creation, inventory checks) to surface within the Copilot Chat interface without requiring users to switch tools.

✍️ Prompt Engineering for M365 Copilot

Effective M365 Copilot prompting follows a consistent framework. Understanding why certain prompt structures work — not just which ones — enables you to adapt them to novel situations.

The GCSE Framework

Microsoft recommends a four-element prompt structure: Goal + Context + Source + Expectations.

Element Purpose How to Write It
Goal What output do you want? Use an imperative verb: "Draft", "Summarize", "Identify", "Compare", "Create a table of..."
Context Who is the audience? What is the situation? "For an executive audience", "For a technical review", "Following our Q3 results call"
Source What data should Copilot reference? Reference specific files with /, people by name, meetings by date/title, or email threads
Expectations Format, length, tone, structure "In 3 bullet points", "Under 200 words", "Professional tone", "With an action items section at the end"

Weak vs. Strong Prompts — Side by Side

Scenario Weak Prompt Strong Prompt
Email draft "Write an email about the project." "Draft a concise follow-up email to the stakeholders from my Q3 project review meeting last Tuesday, summarizing the 3 action items we agreed on and asking for status updates by Friday."
Excel analysis "Analyze my sales data." "In this sales table, identify the top 5 products by revenue growth percentage from January to June, and flag any products with declining sales across 3 or more consecutive months."
Word document "Write a report on AI." "Draft a 2-page executive briefing on our organization's AI readiness based on /AI-Readiness-Assessment.docx, targeting C-suite readers with no technical background. Include a risk summary and 3 recommended next steps."
Teams meeting recap "Summarize the meeting." "From today's product roadmap meeting, extract: (1) decisions made, (2) action items with named owners, (3) open questions that need follow-up. Format as a numbered list."

Advanced Techniques

  • Chaining prompts: Don't try to do everything in one prompt. Generate a first draft → refine it → ask for a different format. Sequential prompts often outperform a single complex prompt.
  • Role assignment: "Act as a senior product manager reviewing this spec. Identify gaps, risks, and missing requirements." — Assigning a role shifts the model's perspective and improves critique quality.
  • Explicit constraints: "Do not include any information from before 2024" or "Only reference the attached document — do not use general knowledge" — explicit exclusions prevent Copilot from mixing sources when precision matters.
  • Iteration over regeneration: If Copilot's output is 80% right, edit the output and ask Copilot to "revise the section on X to be more specific about Y" rather than starting over with a new prompt.

🔑 Licensing & Administration

License Requirements

Microsoft 365 Copilot is an add-on license, not included in standard M365 subscriptions:

  • Required base plan: Microsoft 365 E3, E5, Business Standard, or Business Premium (or equivalent F3 plan for frontline workers with limited Copilot features).
  • Add-on: Microsoft 365 Copilot license per user per month — assigned in Microsoft 365 Admin Center.
  • Copilot Studio add-on: Separate from M365 Copilot — required for building custom copilots in Copilot Studio. Priced per tenant with a message-based consumption model.
⚠️ Copilot features vary by base plan. Some features (like Copilot in Teams meetings with transcript) require specific M365 plans or add-ons beyond the base Copilot license. Verify specific feature availability against your tenant's license configuration.

IT Admin Configuration

  • License assignment: Assign M365 Copilot licenses to users in the Microsoft 365 Admin Center or via Azure AD group-based licensing.
  • Meeting transcription: Must be enabled at the Teams admin policy level before Copilot can generate meeting summaries. Policy: Teams Admin Center → Meetings → Recording & Transcription.
  • Copilot in Teams channels: Requires Teams transcription enabled and may require users to explicitly start Copilot in each meeting — not automatic by default.
  • Data governance: M365 Copilot respects Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels and Data Loss Prevention policies. Files labeled "Highly Confidential" may be excluded from Copilot retrieval based on your DLP rules.
  • Usage analytics: Monitor Copilot adoption in the Microsoft 365 Admin Center → Reports → Copilot Usage. Track active users per app, feature adoption, and usage trends.

Semantic Index Considerations

The Semantic Index that powers Copilot's retrieval is built per-tenant and per-user. For new tenants or newly licensed users, there may be a ramp-up period (days to weeks) before Copilot retrieval quality reaches its full potential as the index is populated. Organizations with large volumes of legacy content may also notice that pre-existing content takes time to be indexed.
